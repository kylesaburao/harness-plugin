# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 writing rules

This reference summarizes all 53 rules and eight general recommendations in Issue 9. Consult the official standard for its full explanations, help text, and examples.

## Section 1: Words

1. **Rule 1.1:** Use words that are approved in the dictionary, permitted technical nouns or verbs, or permitted proper nouns.
2. **Rule 1.2:** Use an approved word only as its specified part of speech.
3. **Rule 1.3:** Use an approved word only with its approved meaning.
4. **Rule 1.4:** Use only approved verb and adjective forms.
5. **Rule 1.5:** Use words that belong to a permitted technical-noun category.
6. **Rule 1.6:** Use an unapproved dictionary word only when it is a technical noun.
7. **Rule 1.7:** Do not use a technical noun as a verb.
8. **Rule 1.8:** Use technical nouns approved by the applicable company, industry, or subject field.
9. **Rule 1.9:** Select technical nouns that are short and easy to understand.
10. **Rule 1.10:** Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.
11. **Rule 1.11:** Do not use different technical nouns for the same item.
12. **Rule 1.12:** Use verbs that belong to a permitted technical-verb category.
13. **Rule 1.13:** Do not use a technical verb as a noun.
14. **Rule 1.14:** Use American English spelling unless an official directive requires different spelling.

## Section 2: Multi-word nouns

15. **Rule 2.1:** Write multi-word nouns with no more than three words.
16. **Rule 2.2:** When an established technical noun has more than three words, write it in full, then introduce an approved shorter form for subsequent use.

## Section 3: Verbs

17. **Rule 3.1:** Use only verb forms listed in the dictionary.
18. **Rule 3.2:** Use only the permitted verb forms and tenses.
19. **Rule 3.3:** Use a past participle as an adjective only when the dictionary permits it.
20. **Rule 3.4:** Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.
21. **Rule 3.5:** Use an `-ing` verb form only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.
22. **Rule 3.6:** Use active voice. In descriptive writing, use passive voice only when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.
23. **Rule 3.7:** Use an approved verb, not a noun or another part of speech, to describe an action.

## Section 4: Sentences

24. **Rule 4.1:** Write short, clear sentences with one topic.
25. **Rule 4.2:** Do not omit words or use contractions to shorten sentences.
26. **Rule 4.3:** Use a vertical list for complex text.
27. **Rule 4.4:** Use approved connecting words and phrases to connect related sentences.
28. **Rule 4.5:** When applicable, use an article or demonstrative adjective before a noun.

## Section 5: Procedural writing

29. **Rule 5.1:** Use no more than 20 words in a sentence.
30. **Rule 5.2:** Put one instruction in each sentence unless two or more actions occur at the same time.
31. **Rule 5.3:** Write instructions in the imperative form.
32. **Rule 5.4:** Put a condition first when the reader must know the condition before the instruction.
33. **Rule 5.5:** Use notes only for information, not instructions.

## Section 6: Descriptive writing

34. **Rule 6.1:** Give information gradually.
35. **Rule 6.2:** Use key words and phrases to give the text a logical structure.
36. **Rule 6.3:** Use no more than 25 words in a sentence.
37. **Rule 6.4:** Use paragraphs to group related information.
38. **Rule 6.5:** Put only one topic in each paragraph.
39. **Rule 6.6:** Put no more than six sentences in a paragraph.

## Section 7: Safety instructions

40. **Rule 7.1:** Identify the risk level with an applicable word, such as `warning` or `caution`.
41. **Rule 7.2:** Start a safety instruction with a clear and accurate command or condition.
42. **Rule 7.3:** Explain the risk or possible result.

## Section 8: Punctuation and word count

43. **Rule 8.1:** Use standard English punctuation, but do not use a semicolon.
44. **Rule 8.2:** Use hyphens to connect directly related words.
45. **Rule 8.3:** Use parentheses only for permitted references, identifiers, abbreviations, forms, explanations, work-step labels, or alternatives.
46. **Rule 8.4:** In a vertical list, treat a colon as a sentence end for word count.
47. **Rule 8.5:** Count parenthetical text as one word.
48. **Rule 8.6:** Count each number, number with unit, abbreviation, alphanumeric identifier, quotation, title, heading, placard, label, and proper noun as one word.
49. **Rule 8.7:** Count a hyphenated word as one word.

## Section 9: Writing practices

50. **Rule 9.1:** Change the sentence construction when word-for-word replacement is not sufficient.
51. **Rule 9.2:** Use every approved word correctly.
52. **Rule 9.3:** Do not make unapproved phrasal verbs by combining words.
53. **Rule 9.4:** Use terminology and wording consistently.

## General recommendations

These recommendations are not STE rules.

1. **GR-1, `that`:** Use `that` after applicable verbs when it makes the boundary of a subordinate clause clear.
2. **GR-2, `with`:** Review every use of `with` for ambiguity. Rewrite the sentence when association, accompaniment, or means is unclear.
3. **GR-3, pronouns:** Use only approved pronouns. Replace an ambiguous pronoun with the noun to which it refers.
4. **GR-4, `this`:** Make sure that `this` has one clear referent. Repeat the applicable noun or context when necessary.
5. **GR-5, false friends:** Confirm that a word has the intended English meaning and not the meaning of a similar word in another language.
6. **GR-6, Latin abbreviations:** Use clear English words instead of Latin abbreviations such as `e.g.`, `i.e.`, and `etc.`.
7. **GR-7, inclusive language:** Use neutral, non-discriminatory, gender-neutral language.
8. **GR-8, possessive form:** Use the possessive form only when it is correct and clear. Use a different construction when uncertain.
